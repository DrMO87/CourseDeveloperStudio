- 2. Wavelength selector:
- The ideal wavelength selector has a high intensity (I0) radiation and a narrow effective bandwidth (nearly monochromatic).
- a) Filters:
- Absorption filters work by selectively absorbing undesired radiation from the electromagnetic radiation and transmits the desired part.
- Interference filters use constructive and destructive interference to isolate undesired radiation from the electromagnetic radiation and transmits the desired part.
- Advantages of filters: Simple, cheap and stable.
- Disadvantages of filters: One filter can isolate only one band of wavelengths (i.e. filters do not have variable wavelength settings) so they are used only when measurements are made at a fixed wavelength.

- 4. Detector:
- Photo multipliers:
- In order to obtain greater sensitivity to very weak light, multiplication of the initial photoelectrons (i.e. electrons emitted from the cathode after exposure to light) by employing secondary emission processes.
- Principle (Mechanism):
- The photomultiplier contains several anodes of a gradual increasing positive potential called “Dynodes” present in one bulb.
- The final resulting current is 106-108 times greater than the primary current.
- By placing a photodiode array along the monochromator’s focal plane, it is possible to monitor simultaneously an entire range of wavelengths.

- 2. Double-Beam Spectrophotometer
- instrument that has two light paths one for the blank and one for the sample.
- The chopper controls the radiation’s path, alternating it between the sample, the blank, and a shutter.
- Advantages:
- automated recording of absorption spectrum and more accurate
- Disadvantages:
- more expensive
- Examples of molecular UV/Vis instruments

#### Slide 15
- 3.  Diode array Spectrometer
- An instrument where photomultiplier is replaced with many photodiodes, then we can use the resulting array of detectors to record an entire spectrum simultaneously in as little as 0.1 s
- Advantages:
- small, compact and speed of data acquisition, which allows to collect several spectra for a single sample.
- Examples of molecular UV/Vis instruments

- 1. Effect of pH on the absorption spectrum
- At different pH, the spectrum will be shifted to different λmax but all spectra intersect at certain  which is known as isosbestic point
- At isosbestic point, absorbance is not pH dependent but concentration dependent ,i.e the same absorbance is given for the same concentration at different pH.
- Thus for solution ; whose λmax is affected by pH, it must be buffered (to adjust pH at constant value) or measurements are carried out at the λ of isosbestic point.

- Applications of UV-Vis spectroscopy
- Impurities can cause distortion of the absorption spectrum of the analyte leading to either over estimation (i.e.higher absorbance); or to shifting of λmax.
- Impurities are detected through calculating: Impurity index (I.I.)
- The impurity index increases as the purity decrease & equal to zero if sample is 100% pure.
- Absorptivity (ε) and λ max of U.V and visible absorption spectrum usually give fingerprint of the sample to be analyzed, hence could be used for its identification.
