#### Slide 3
- Molecular UV/Vis Spectroscopy
- It is the study of the interaction between electromagnetic radiation (EMR) and molecules and atoms of a matter.
- It can be used for identification of materials (qualitative analysis) as well as determination of their amounts (quantitative analysis).

#### Slide 4
- Electromagnetic radiation
- Electromagnetic radiation has the properties of both particles and waves. and consists of magnetic and electric fields and both are perpendicular on each other and on the direction of radiation propagation.
- Light is a part of Electromagnetic radiation.
- In a vaccum electromagnetic radiation travels at the speed of light, c, which is 3.00 × 108 m/s

#### Slide 5
- Wave nature of EMR
- Wavelength (λ): the distance between two successive maxima or minima.  (Unit m, cm, µm, nm, …..).
- Frequency (ν): the number of oscillations per second (Unit s-1, Hz).
- Wavenumber(): the reciprocal of wavelength or the number of waves per cm (Unit cm-1).
- Particle nature of EMR
- radiation consists of a beam of energetic particles called photons.
- When a photon is absorbed by a sample it is “destroyed,” and its energy acquired by the sample.
- N.B. ↑ → ↓ E → ↓ →↓

#### Slide 10
- Interaction of EMR and matter
- Absorption
- When molecules are subjected to EMR in UV/Vis region, some of them absorb a portion of light energy
- as a result, electrons undergo transition from ground state (E1) to excited state (E2).
- Emission
- If the electrons return from excited state to ground state (relaxation process) directly then heat will evolve
- if the electrons relaxed from excited state to ground state via second excited state then light will be emitted (Fluorescence and phosphorescence).
- ΔE = E2 – E1 = hν (quantum)
- N.B. Absorbing large amount of energy may lead to bond rupture and new compounds being formed. This phenomenon is described as photolysis.

#### Slide 11
- Types of electronic transition
- The valence electrons in organic molecules occupy quantized sigma bonding, σ, pi bonding, π, and non-bonding, n, molecular orbitals (MOs).
- Because the difference in energy between the highest-energy occupied MOs and the lowest-energy unoccupied MOs corresponds to ultraviolet and visible radiation, absorption of a photon is possible.
- Transition
- Wavelength Range
- Examples
- σ → σ∗
- <200 nm
- C–C, C–H (bond breaking)
- n → σ∗
- 160–260 nm
- H2O, CH3OH, CH3Cl
- π → π∗
- 200–500 nm
- C=C, C=O, C=N, C≡C
- n → π∗
- 250–600 nm
- C=O, C=N, N=N, N=O

#### Slide 12
- Types of electronic transition
- Since E α 1/λ therefore, light with shorter wavelength will have higher energy leading to the excitation of electrons from σ → σ∗ and this will lead to bond breaking.
- The most frequently used transition is the π → π∗ transition
- Compounds containing heteroatoms N, S, O or halogens contain non-bonding electrons that can be easily excited to σ∗ or π∗ with lower energy i.e. with radiations of longer wavelength lying in the visible light range, and that is why those compounds are colored.
- e.g. benzene is colorless, while nitrobenzene is yellow

#### Slide 13
- The relation between spectra and chemical structure
- Absorbance of EMR by organic molecule is achieved by chromophoric groups (chromophores) assisted by auxochromes.
- What is a chromophore?
- It is unsaturated organic group responsible for electronic absorption e.g. Carbonyl (C=O), Azo (-N=N-), Nitro (-NO2), conjugated diene (C=C-C=C) and aromatic ring
- What is an auxochrome?
- It is a saturated organic group which when attached to a chromophore, changes both the λ and intensity of absorption maxima e.g. Amino (-NH2), Hydroxyl (-OH)

#### Slide 14
- The relation between spectra and chemical structure
- N.B. All auxochromes have one or more non-bonding pair of electrons. If an auxochrome is attached to a chromophore, it extends the conjugation by sharing of non-bonding electrons.

#### Slide 15
- Absorption spectrum
- Absorption spectrum is the plot of Absorbance (A) as a function of wavelength (λ).
- λ max is characteristic for each molecule, therefore it is used for identification of a chemical substance (i.e. qualitative analysis).
- Also, λ max is used for quantitative measurement, in order to increase sensitivity and to minimize error of the analytical method.

#### Slide 16
- Transmittance and Absorbance
- Transmittance (T) is the ratio of the source radiation’s power exiting the sample, PT, to that incident on the sample, P0.
- Multiplying the transmittance by 100 gives the percent transmittance, (%T) (Ranges 100%-0%)
- Absorbance (A) is the more common unit for expressing the attenuation of radiation because it is a linear function of the analyte’s concentration.
- x100

#### Slide 17
- Absorbance and Concentration: Beer’s Law
- When a monochromatic light having intensity (P0) is allowed to pass through absorbing medium, some is reflected (Pr) (usually eliminated), some is absorbed (Pa), and some is transmitted (Pt)
- Beer-Lambert's law
- The absorbance is directly proportional to the concentration (c) of the absorbing species in a sample and the pathlength (b) of the absorbing medium.
- A = constant x b x c
- Po = Pa + Pt

#### Slide 19
- Limitations to Beer’s lawIn many cases a calibration curve deviates from this ideal behavior:
- Real deviation
- At very high concentrations (more than about 0.01 M) due to decrease of distances between particles that will affect extent of absorption.

#### Slide 20
- Limitations to Beer’s lawIn many cases a calibration curve deviates from this ideal behavior:
- Chemical deviation
- It occurs when the absorbing compound undergoes a chemical reaction with the solvent that gives a product which absorbs differently from the original compound.
- Ex.: Unbuffered solution of dichromate (Cr2O72-) changes its color from orange to yellow upon dilution with water due to its conversion into chromate (CrO42-) as given by the following equilibrium:
- Cr2O72- (Orange, 440 nm) + H2O ⇌ 2 H+ +2 CrO42- (Yellow, 390 nm)

#### Slide 21
- Limitations to Beer’s lawIn many cases a calibration curve deviates from this ideal behavior:
- Instrumental deviation
- a) Unmatched or Unclean cells (cuvettes).
- b) Use of polychromatic radiation instead of monochromatic.
- c) Stray light (any light reaching the detector outside the specified wavelength band due to scattering, reflection, instrumental problem  etc).
